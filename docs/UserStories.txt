MVP user stories and acceptance criteria
Epic 1: Today Dashboard
US01: View today's care picture
As a family coordinator, I want to see today's medication, visits, tasks and alerts in one place so that I can quickly understand whether everything is on track.
Acceptance criteria
Given I open Margaret's dashboard, when today's information loads, then I can see:
medication due today and its status
scheduled visits/check-ins
outstanding tasks
active alerts
recent activity
Given an item is complete, then it is visually shown as green.
Given an item requires attention, then it is visually shown as amber.
Given an item requires urgent human attention, then it is visually shown as red.
The dashboard shows the last recorded status rather than assuming an activity occurred.
I can navigate from an alert or outstanding item to its details.
Epic 2: Medication Tracker
US02: Add a medication
As a family coordinator, I want to record Margaret's medication schedule so that the care circle knows what medication support is expected.
Acceptance criteria
I can enter a medication name, schedule and reminder time.
The medication appears on Margaret's Medication Tracker.
Scheduled medication appears on the Today Dashboard when relevant.
Adding or changing medication creates an Activity Feed entry.
The prototype does not provide dosing advice or make medication recommendations.
US03: Record medication status
As a carer or family member, I want to record whether a scheduled medication was taken so that other people don't have to rely on WhatsApp messages or verbal handovers.
Acceptance criteria
For each scheduled medication event I can record:
Taken
Missed
Declined
Unknown
The system records who made the update and when.
The latest status is visible on the Today Dashboard.
The change is recorded in the Activity Feed.
If no status has been recorded within the configured prototype threshold, the status becomes Unknown and the alert rules are evaluated.
The system never tells Margaret whether she should take or skip medication.
Epic 3: Visits & Tasks
US04: Schedule a visit
As a family coordinator, I want to schedule a visit or wellbeing check and assign it to someone so that everybody knows who is responsible.
Acceptance criteria
I can create a visit containing:
date
time
visit/check-in type
assigned person
optional notes
The visit appears on the Today Dashboard when due.
The assigned person's name is clearly displayed.
Creating or changing the visit generates an Activity Feed entry.
US05: Complete a visit
As a carer or trusted volunteer, I want to record the outcome of my assigned visit so that the rest of the care circle knows what happened.
Acceptance criteria
I can mark an assigned visit as complete.
I can add a short note.
I can record whether there is a concern requiring attention.
The system records who updated the visit and when.
The update appears in the Activity Feed.
A trusted volunteer sees only the information required for their assigned visit/task.
US06: Identify a missed visit
As a family coordinator, I want to know when a scheduled visit hasn't been completed so that I can check whether follow-up is required.
Acceptance criteria
Given a visit's expected completion time has passed, and it has not been completed, then the rules engine generates an alert.
The alert explains which visit was missed and when.
The alert identifies the person responsible for responding.
AI may explain the coordination issue but does not determine that neglect has occurred.
Epic 4: Care Circle
US07: View the care circle
As a member of Margaret's care circle, I want to know who is involved and what their role is so that I know who to contact when something needs attention.
Acceptance criteria
I can see Margaret as the supported person.
I can see her:
daughter/family coordinator
carer
trusted volunteer
escalation/emergency contacts
Each person has a clearly displayed role.
Appropriate contact information is available according to role.
US08: Switch roles for the prototype
As a demo user, I want to switch between care-circle roles so that I can demonstrate what different users would experience.
Acceptance criteria
I can switch between:
Supported Person
Family Coordinator
Carer
Trusted Volunteer
The interface clearly displays the currently selected role.
Switching roles changes the information/actions available where relevant.
No production authentication is required.
Epic 5: Alerts & AI
US09: Generate an alert from an unresolved medication event
As a family coordinator, I want to be alerted when Margaret's medication status remains unknown so that I know something needs checking.
Acceptance criteria
Given medication was expected, when its configured check window expires without a recorded outcome, then a rules-based alert is generated.
The alert records:
what happened
when it happened
why the rule triggered
who should respond
The alert is visible on the Today Dashboard.
The alert also appears in the Activity Feed.
Alert generation is deterministic/rules-based rather than decided by AI.
US10: Understand an alert
As a family coordinator, I want an AI-generated plain-English explanation so that I can quickly understand why something needs attention.
Acceptance criteria
The explanation uses only information already recorded in CareCircle.
It clearly distinguishes recorded facts from a suggested next step.
Suggested actions are operational/non-clinical, for example:
call Margaret
message Margaret
contact another care-circle member
AI does not:
diagnose a condition
recommend treatment
recommend medication dosage
tell Margaret to take or skip medication
determine that abuse or neglect has occurred
The UI makes clear that a human decides what to do next.
US11: Prioritise multiple outstanding issues
As a family coordinator, I want to see which coordination issues most need my attention so that I'm not forced to reconstruct the situation from multiple updates.
Acceptance criteria
Active alerts can be displayed in priority order.
Priority is based on defined prototype rules, such as unresolved/multiple events, not clinical judgement.
The reason an item has been prioritised can be explained in plain English.
Red status represents urgent human attention, not a clinical diagnosis.
Epic 6: Acknowledge & Escalate
US12: Acknowledge an alert
As Margaret's daughter, I want to acknowledge an alert so that the care circle knows somebody is responding.
Acceptance criteria
I can select Acknowledge on an active alert.
The alert displays:
who acknowledged it
acknowledgement time
Other care-circle members can see that somebody is responding.
An acknowledgement is added to the Activity Feed.
Acknowledging an alert does not automatically mark the underlying issue as resolved.
US13: Record an unsuccessful contact attempt
As Margaret's daughter, I want to record that I tried but failed to contact Margaret so that other people have an accurate handover.
Acceptance criteria
I can record an attempted contact.
I can select an outcome of No response / unable to contact.
The attempt records who made it and when.
The unsuccessful attempt appears in the Activity Feed.
The alert remains unresolved.
US14: Escalate an unresolved alert
As a family coordinator, I want an unresolved issue to escalate to the next contact so that it doesn't disappear after the first person responds unsuccessfully.
Acceptance criteria
The prototype has a predefined escalation sequence.
Given the primary responder cannot resolve the issue, when escalation is triggered, then the next nominated contact is identified.
A simulated notification is created.
The escalation is shown in the Activity Feed.
The system never automatically contacts NHS 111 or 999.
A human remains responsible for deciding whether professional or emergency support is required.
Epic 7: Resolution
US15: Resolve an alert
As a family coordinator, I want to mark an issue resolved once Margaret has been successfully contacted so that everyone can see that no further coordination action is outstanding.
Acceptance criteria
I can record Contact successful.
I can add a short outcome note.
I can mark the alert resolved.
The system records who resolved it and when.
The alert disappears from the outstanding-alert view but remains available in history.
Resolution is added to the Activity Feed.
The UI does not infer medical wellbeing simply from successful contact.
Epic 8: Shared Activity & Handover
US16: View recent activity
As a member of the care circle, I want a shared chronological record of recent care activity so that I don't need to reconstruct events from WhatsApp, a whiteboard and conversations.
Acceptance criteria
Activity is displayed chronologically.
Each relevant entry shows:
who
what happened
when
why, where relevant
outcome, where relevant
Medication updates, visits, contact attempts, alerts, acknowledgements, escalations and resolutions are recorded.
Entries clearly distinguish recorded actions from AI-generated explanation.
US17: Understand recent history before a handover
As a family member or carer, I want a concise summary of recent activity so that I can understand what has happened before supporting Margaret or speaking to a healthcare professional.
Acceptance criteria
AI can summarise recorded recent activity.
The summary is based only on information stored in CareCircle.
Important unresolved coordination issues are highlighted.
The original activity entries remain accessible.
The summary does not introduce diagnoses, clinical interpretation or information that wasn't recorded.
Epic 9: Safety & Accessibility
US18: Understand CareCircle's boundaries
As any user, I want it to be clear what CareCircle can and cannot do so that I don't mistake coordination guidance for medical advice.
Acceptance criteria
The product clearly displays:
CareCircle AI helps coordinate support. It does not provide medical advice or replace professional or emergency services.
The safety message is visible around AI-generated content and escalation experiences.
AI recommendations are labelled as suggestions for human consideration.
CareCircle does not automatically make care decisions or contact emergency services.
US19: Use CareCircle easily
As an older or busy user, I want CareCircle to be simple and accessible so that I can understand what's happening without learning a complicated system.
Acceptance criteria
Primary actions use clear, plain-English labels.
Important information does not rely on colour alone.
Statuses include both colour and text/icon indicators.
Interactive controls use large touch targets, targeting at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels.
Text remains readable at increased browser zoom.
Critical journeys can be completed without relying on hover interactions.
Navigation remains consistent between screens.
End-to-end demo acceptance scenario
I'd make this the single golden path for the hackathon demo rather than trying to demonstrate every story independently.
Scenario: Margaret's medication isn't confirmed
Given
Margaret has been onboarded.
Her daughter is Family Coordinator.
A carer and trusted volunteer have joined her care circle.
Margaret has a scheduled medication.
A volunteer visit has also been scheduled.
When
The medication's expected check window passes without confirmation.
Its status becomes Unknown.
A rules-based alert is triggered.
AI explains: “Margaret's medication status has not been confirmed. This needs checking because no outcome has been recorded.”
Margaret's daughter acknowledges the alert.
She attempts to call Margaret.
She records Unable to contact.
The alert remains unresolved and is escalated to the backup contact.
Margaret is subsequently contacted successfully.
Her daughter records the outcome and resolves the alert.
Then
The dashboard returns to an appropriate non-alert state.
The full sequence remains visible in the Activity Feed.
The feed shows who did what, when, why and the outcome.
No clinical conclusion is generated.
The human care circle remains responsible for every action.
Suggested prototype backlog
For a less-than-one-day build, I would prioritise:
P0: Must demoUS01, US03, US04, US05, US07, US08, US09, US10, US12, US13, US14, US15, US16, US18.
P1: Add if time allowsUS02, US06, US11, US17, US19.
The most important product test is whether the prototype can make the chain “what happened → who knows → who is acting → what happened next” immediately understandable. That addresses the heart of your problem statement rather than turning the prototype into a general-purpose care management system.
